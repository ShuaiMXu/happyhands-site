## 概述

Sellix is a focused fintech landing-page hero for cross-border payments, asset custody and crypto-to-fiat on/off-ramping. It uses a looping violet globe to establish trust and global reach, then places a direct value proposition and two clear calls to action above the fold. The whole concept is self-contained, making it a useful reference for teams that need a polished first screen before building the rest of a financial product site.

## 布局

**Global-finance visual language** — The rotating globe and starfield immediately connect the product to international money movement while keeping the page focused on one promise.

**Centred headline and short supporting copy** — A two-line headline carries the message, with a compact paragraph underneath so visitors can understand the offer without scanning a full page.

**Two actions with a clear hierarchy** — A bright primary action and a quieter secondary action make the next step obvious for both ready-to-buy visitors and people who want more context.

**Responsive single-viewport composition** — The design is measured from one reference unit so the hero scales as a composed scene instead of becoming a loose stack of unrelated blocks.


## 动效

**Looping globe background** — The silent violet globe clip supplies a calm, continuous sense of movement without competing with the headline or the calls to action.

**Masked headline reveal** — Each headline line rises into view independently, giving the first screen a premium entrance while leaving the final state easy to read.

## 文案规则

**Why this template works for fintech** — Financial products often have a trust problem before they have a feature problem. A visitor needs to understand that the product is global, serious and easy to start with, but a wall of compliance language or product terminology can make the first screen feel heavy. Sellix solves that tension with a single visual idea: a luminous globe that makes cross-border movement immediately legible. The copy then stays plain and operational. Payments, custody and on/off-ramping are named in a short supporting paragraph instead of being hidden behind abstract brand language. This makes the template especially useful for neobanks, payment infrastructure, treasury platforms, remittance products and digital-asset tools that need to communicate reach without looking like a trading terminal.

**How the first screen guides attention** — The page has a deliberate reading order. Navigation establishes the product and gives returning visitors a familiar way to move around. The central headline states the category-level promise in two lines, while the supporting sentence adds the concrete jobs the product can perform. The primary button is visually quiet enough to belong to the scene but bright enough to be found immediately; the secondary action gives a more cautious visitor a lower-pressure route. Nothing else competes with that path. There is no card grid, testimonial wall or decorative dashboard before the first decision. That restraint is valuable when adapting the template for a new brand, because it gives you a clear test: if a new visual or sentence does not help explain the offer or the next action, it probably belongs below the fold.

**Responsive behavior and accessibility** — The original brief treats the desktop hero as a measured composition rather than a collection of arbitrary breakpoints. On a wide screen, the globe fills the stage and the headline remains centred against it. As the viewport narrows, the type is sized from the available width so the long category phrase does not break in an awkward place. Navigation folds into a compact menu, controls remain large enough to tap, and the supporting copy is allowed to wrap naturally instead of being forced into tiny text. The video is muted, plays inline and has a poster frame, so the page still communicates its idea when autoplay is unavailable. A reduced-motion preference disables the entrance and holds the first frame, preserving the message for visitors who do not want a moving background.

**Adapting the reference to your own brand** — Start by changing the promise, not the decoration. Write one sentence that names the transaction or outcome your product makes easier, then choose a supporting line that clarifies who it is for. After that, replace the globe with a loop that carries a similar amount of information: a product close-up, a route map, a material study or a calm data visualization can all work if it has a stable focal area behind the type. Keep the contrast relationship intact and test the headline over the brightest frame, not only the poster. Finally, rewrite the navigation and calls to action around the real journey you want people to take. The value of this template is the measured hierarchy and pacing; your brand should supply the subject, proof and language.
